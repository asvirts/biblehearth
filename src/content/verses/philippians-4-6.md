### What is Paul asking the church to do?

In Philippians 4:6, Paul invites the church to bring its concerns to God in
prayer. He names requests and thanksgiving alongside prayer, giving readers a
way to speak honestly about what they need and what they can still be grateful
for. A petition is simply a request; it need not sound polished or impressive.

The next sentence belongs with the first. In
[Philippians 4:7](/verse/philippians/4/7), Paul speaks of God's peace guarding
hearts and minds in Christ Jesus. The image is of protection, not a timetable
for when a difficult situation will be resolved.

### Why does the King James Version say “be careful”?

The King James Version uses “careful” in an older sense connected with being
full of care or worry. The New International Version speaks of being “anxious,”
and the New International Reader's Version uses “worry.” These choices help modern readers
hear the concern Paul addresses. He is not forbidding people to make plans,
take sensible precautions, or care about others.

The translations also handle the prayer language differently. The NIV names
prayer and petition; the NIrV explains the invitation in more everyday terms.
Each directs the reader toward bringing concerns and gratitude to God. You can
compare the available translations above, alongside their publisher notices;
the KJV is linked below as a historical comparison.

### What was happening in Philippi?

Paul writes while imprisoned, though the letter does not name the city where he
is held. The church has sent him a gift through Epaphroditus. Earlier in the
chapter, Paul asks Euodia and Syntyche to agree and invites a companion to help
them. He speaks about prayer to a real community dealing with relationships,
need, and separation, rather than to people whose lives are free of pressure.

Read [Philippians 4:5](/verse/philippians/4/5) immediately before the verse and
[the full chapter study](/philippians/4) for the surrounding appeals to
rejoice, show gentleness, and care for one another.

### Does feeling anxious mean I have failed God?

The verse gives a spiritual practice; it does not diagnose an anxiety disorder
or say that every anxious feeling is a moral failure. Severe or persistent
anxiety deserves compassionate support. Prayer can accompany conversations with
trusted people, counseling, medication, and other care recommended by a
qualified professional. If gratitude feels difficult, a person can begin by
naming the need without pretending the pain is gone.
