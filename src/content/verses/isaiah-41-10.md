### What does the promise mean?

In Isaiah 41:10, God speaks to Israel as a people facing threats and uncertainty.
He gives two reasons for courage: he is with them and he is their God. He then
promises to strengthen, help, and uphold them. The verse describes God's support
for a vulnerable community; it does not ask people to invent their own strength.

The image of God's right hand points to his power to act. Calling that hand
righteous connects its strength with God's just and faithful character. In
[Isaiah 41:13](/verse/isaiah/41/13), the picture becomes more intimate: God
holds his people's right hand and repeats the promise to help.

### Why do translations use different words?

The New International Version says not to be “dismayed,” while the Berean
Standard Bible says not to be “afraid.” Both describe a response to danger and
place God's presence alongside the instruction. The two renderings do not
change who is speaking or remove the promises that follow.

Some English Bibles use older forms such as “thee.” In this passage the singular
address speaks to Israel, named in [verse 8](/verse/isaiah/41/8) and described
as God's servant in [verse 9](/verse/isaiah/41/9). A singular word in English
can sound like an address to one isolated person, so the surrounding verses
help us hear the community being addressed.

### Read it in the chapter's setting

Isaiah 41 belongs to a larger section that speaks of exile and the hope of
restoration. The chapter opens with the nations summoned to a hearing. It
then names Israel and Jacob, recalls God's choice, and offers reassurance amid
opposition. The next verses describe enemies who contend with the people; they
make clear that the command to fear less is spoken in the presence of danger.

Later, God promises water to people in a dry place and challenges the idols to
show what they can do. Those scenes develop the same question: where can a
frightened community find help? The full [Isaiah 41 study](/isaiah/41) follows
that movement through the chapter.

### Can I draw comfort from this verse today?

Christian readers often find comfort here in God's presence and care. Remembering
the promise's first audience helps us receive it without treating it as a
prediction that every personal difficulty will end in a particular way. Fear
can be named honestly, and seeking support from trusted people and qualified
professionals is compatible with prayer.
