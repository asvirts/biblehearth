### Who is the Word in John 1:14?

John's opening identifies the Word with the one who becomes known as Jesus
Christ. [John 1:1](/verse/john/1/1) says the Word was with God and was God;
[John 1:17](/verse/john/1/17) names Jesus Christ. The opening passage moves from
the Word's life before creation to his life among people. Christians call this
the *incarnation*: God the Son becoming human.

The verse does not present Jesus as a message sent from a distance. It says
the Word entered human life. John goes on to tell of Jesus speaking, meeting
people, suffering, dying, and rising again.

### What does “the Word became flesh” mean?

The NIV is among the translations displayed above. The KJV and NLT comparisons
here link to their publisher-hosted texts under Sources and further reading.

“Flesh” here means human life in its physical, vulnerable condition. John is
saying that the Word truly became human, not merely that he appeared human. The
New International Version says “became flesh”; the New Living Translation
renders it “became human.” Both introduce the same claim in language suited to
their readers. The King James Version uses the older wording “was made flesh.”

Christians understand this verse together with John 1:1: the Word who was with
God and was God enters human life. It does not say that God stopped being God
when the Word became flesh.

### What does “dwelt among us” mean?

The King James Version says “dwelt among us,” while the NIV says “made his
dwelling among us” and the NLT says “made his home among us.” Each describes
the Word living among people. The Greek verb is related to a word for a tent;
many readers hear an echo of the tent where God's presence dwelt among Israel
in the wilderness. That connection enriches the image without requiring Jesus
to have lived in a literal tent.

### What are glory, grace, and truth?

John says that “we” have seen the Son's glory: the revealing of who he is and
his relationship to the Father. The chapter calls him full of grace and truth.
Grace is God's generous favor; truth speaks of what is reliable and real. The
NLT uses “unfailing love and faithfulness” to express those qualities. In the
rest of the Gospel, readers see them in Jesus' words and actions.

Read [John 1:14 in context](/bible/niv/john/1) alongside
[John 1:18](/verse/john/1/18), where the Son makes God known. The opening of
John invites readers to follow that claim through the story of Jesus.
