### What does it mean to serve two masters?

In Matthew 6:24, Jesus says a person cannot serve God and money as competing
masters. His image is of a servant whose loyalties are claimed by two owners.
When their demands conflict, the servant cannot give each the same devotion.
Jesus uses that picture to ask which allegiance governs a person's life.

Money can provide food, shelter, and care for others. Jesus is speaking about
what happens when it becomes the thing a person trusts and obeys above God.
The question reaches beyond a bank balance to the choices people make with
what they have.

### What is mammon?

The New International Version uses the word “money.” The Amplified Bible uses
“mammon” and adds an explanation in brackets that includes money and possessions.
Mammon is an older word associated with wealth. The Amplified Bible's bracketed
examples are its way of expanding the idea for readers; the verse's central
contrast remains between service to God and service to wealth.

The language of love and hate is deliberately strong. Jesus is describing
rival loyalties, not suggesting that every financial decision proves a person
has chosen one master forever.

### How does this fit the Sermon on the Mount?

Jesus teaches these words during the Sermon on the Mount, with his disciples
and the gathered crowds in view. Earlier in Matthew 6 he speaks about giving,
prayer, and storing treasure. In [Matthew 6:21](/verse/matthew/6/21), he connects
a person's treasure with the heart. The picture of the two masters brings that
concern about devotion into focus.

Immediately afterward, Jesus speaks about worry over food, drink, and clothing.
Those needs are real in the passage: he names them while encouraging his
listeners to seek God's kingdom. Read the [Matthew 6 study](/matthew/6) for the
whole movement from treasure to trust.

### Does Jesus forbid saving or earning money?

This verse does not give a rule against having a job, saving for a need, or
planning a budget. It asks what controls those decisions. A person might ask
whether the pursuit of more is crowding out generosity, honest work, or care
for family and neighbors. Such questions can be asked with humility whether
someone has a little or a great deal.

The [Bible verses about money](/topics/money) bring this teaching into
conversation with other passages about provision and generosity.
