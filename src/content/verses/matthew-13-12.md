### What is Jesus talking about?

In Matthew 13:12, Jesus speaks about having and receiving more in the context
of understanding his message about the kingdom of heaven. His disciples have
asked why he teaches the crowds in parables, or stories that use familiar
images to convey a deeper meaning. Jesus answers that knowledge of the kingdom
has been given to them, then gives this difficult saying about having and losing.

The words can sound like a rule that the rich should get richer. The nearby
verses point the reader toward a different subject: hearing and understanding
what Jesus is teaching. The verse speaks of both further understanding and a
serious loss; its warning should not be softened away.

### Why do translations sound different?

The New International Version leaves the object of “has” unstated in verse 12.
The New International Reader's Version makes the connection with knowledge
explicit. The Amplified Bible adds bracketed explanation about spiritual wisdom
and response to God's word. Those additions help readers follow an interpretation
of the sentence; they also show why it is useful to read the surrounding verses
alongside a translation's explanatory wording.

### Why did Jesus speak in parables?

Before this exchange, Jesus tells a crowd beside the lake a story about seed
falling on different kinds of ground. The disciples then ask him about his use
of stories. Jesus describes people who see and hear without understanding and
quotes Isaiah's words about a people whose hearts have grown unresponsive.

The passage holds together a gift of insight to the disciples and a warning
about resistant hearing. Christians have differed over how to understand the
relationship between God's giving and human response here. The text itself
invites readers to take both seriously without claiming to settle every question
about why one person understands and another does not.

Jesus goes on to explain the seed story to the disciples. In
[Matthew 13:23](/verse/matthew/13/23), the good soil represents someone who
hears and understands the message and bears fruit. Read the surrounding
[Matthew 13 chapter](/matthew/13) to follow the story and its explanation.

### What if I am struggling to understand?

The disciples themselves ask questions and receive an explanation. Difficulty
with a passage is an invitation to keep listening, ask for help, and read the
story in its setting. This verse gives no reason to measure someone's worth by
wealth or to assume that a sincere question means God has rejected them.
