shortAnswer: >-
  The Bible's instructions to parents are mostly about restraint. Ephesians and
  Colossians both tell fathers what not to do — do not provoke, do not embitter
  — and Deuteronomy locates formation in ordinary daily conversation rather than
  formal instruction. Proverbs 22:6 is the most quoted verse here and is a
  proverb, meaning a general pattern rather than a guarantee.

misuse: >-
  Proverbs 22:6 is routinely read as a guarantee that good parenting produces
  faithful adults, which leaves parents of struggling children carrying a
  verdict Scripture never issued. Proverbs states general patterns; the same
  book says diligence brings wealth, and Job exists. Read it as wisdom about
  the durability of early formation, not a contract. The more serious misuse is
  "spare the rod": Proverbs 13:24 has been used to justify harming children,
  and it cannot bear that weight next to Ephesians 6:4 and Colossians 3:21,
  where the explicit concerns are exasperation and discouragement. Any reading
  of a proverb that produces a frightened child contradicts the New Testament
  instructions aimed squarely at parents.

It is worth noticing how little the New Testament tells parents to do, and how
pointedly it tells them what to avoid. Both direct instructions to fathers are
warnings, and both name the child's discouragement as the thing at stake.

Deuteronomy fills in the positive side, and it is similarly unspectacular:
talk about these things while walking around, at meals, at bedtime. Repetition
in ordinary hours, not a curriculum.
