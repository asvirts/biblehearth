shortAnswer: >-
  The Bible's instructions to parents are mostly about restraint. Ephesians and
  Colossians warn fathers against provoking or embittering their children.
  Deuteronomy places formation within ordinary daily conversation. Proverbs
  22:6, the best-known parenting verse, offers a wisdom pattern about early
  formation, not a guarantee about every child's future.

misuse: >-
  Proverbs 22:6 is routinely read as a guarantee that good parenting produces
  faithful adults, which leaves parents of struggling children carrying a
  verdict Scripture never issued. Proverbs states general patterns; the same
  book says diligence brings wealth, and Job exists. Read it as wisdom about
  the lasting influence of early formation. The more serious misuse is
  "spare the rod": Proverbs 13:24 has been used to justify harming children,
  and that reading conflicts with Ephesians 6:4 and Colossians 3:21,
  where the explicit concerns are exasperation and discouragement. Any reading
  of a proverb that produces a frightened child contradicts the New Testament
  instructions aimed squarely at parents.

The New Testament's direct instructions to fathers are brief and pointed. Both
warn parents to avoid patterns that exasperate, embitter, or discourage their
children.

Deuteronomy supplies a positive picture through ordinary routines. Parents talk
about faith while sitting at home, walking, going to bed, and getting up.
Formation grows through repeated attention in everyday hours.
