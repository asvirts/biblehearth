shortAnswer: >-
  The first thing Genesis calls "not good" is a person being alone, before sin
  enters the story. Scripture treats isolation as a real human problem. Its
  answer includes both God's enduring presence and the company of other people.
  Elijah felt certain that he was the only faithful person left, though seven
  thousand others still remained.

misuse: >-
  The most common misuse is "God is all you need," offered to lonely people as
  though wanting human company were a spiritual deficiency. Genesis says the
  opposite in the second chapter, before anything is broken, and Psalm 68
  pictures God's response to loneliness as placing people in households.
  Loneliness also isn't evidence that someone has
  done something wrong. Elijah felt utterly alone directly after his greatest
  success. He had incomplete information, not hidden guilt. These verses invite
  people to seek safe community and reject the idea that silent isolation is a
  mark of stronger faith.

      families.
  - q: Does God promise never to leave us?
    a: >-
      Deuteronomy 31:6 gives that promise to Israel facing an intimidating task,
      and Hebrews 13:5 applies it to individuals. Matthew closes with Jesus
      saying he is with his followers always, to the very end of the age. The
      promise is continuing presence through difficult circumstances.

Genesis 1 is a chapter of things being good. Genesis 2 introduces the first
exception: a person is alone.

That placement matters whenever loneliness is treated as a spiritual problem
that prayer should quickly remove. Scripture's answer includes a God who
promises to remain and people who can offer tangible company.
