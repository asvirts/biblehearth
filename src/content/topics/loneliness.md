shortAnswer: >-
  The first thing called "not good" in Genesis is a person being alone — before
  anything has gone wrong. Scripture treats isolation as a genuine problem
  rather than a spiritual test, and answers it in two ways at once: a promise of
  presence that does not depend on circumstances, and actual people. Elijah, at
  his lowest, was convinced he was the only one left and was factually mistaken.

misuse: >-
  The most common misuse is "God is all you need," offered to lonely people as
  though wanting human company were a spiritual deficiency. Genesis says the
  opposite in the second chapter, before anything is broken, and Psalm 68
  pictures God's response to loneliness as placing people in households — not
  as making company unnecessary. Loneliness is also not evidence that you have
  done something wrong. Elijah felt utterly alone directly after his greatest
  success, and was mistaken about the facts rather than guilty of anything.
  These verses are an invitation to reach for people, not a reason to endure
  isolation quietly and call it faith.

Genesis 1 is a chapter of things being good. Genesis 2 introduces the first
exception, and it is not sin — it is a person by himself.

That placement is worth holding onto when loneliness gets treated as a
spiritual problem to be prayed away. Scripture's own answer runs in two
directions at once: a God who promises not to leave, and other people.
