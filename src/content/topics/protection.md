  Scripture pictures God's protection as a refuge, tower, shadow, and shield.
  These images offer shelter without promising immunity from every harm. Satan
  quotes Psalm 91 to urge Jesus to test God's protection, and Jesus rejects that
  use of the psalm. Across these passages, God remains present with people who
  travel through real danger and suffering.

misuse: >-
  Psalm 91 is the clearest case of misuse in Scripture, because Scripture
  itself contains the correction. Satan quotes it to Jesus as proof he can
  throw himself from the temple safely, and Jesus rejects the suggestion as
  God to the test. Read as a guarantee against accident, illness or violence,
  the psalm sets people up to conclude that God failed them or that their faith
  was insufficient. The
  same Bible records Stephen stoned, James executed, Paul beaten repeatedly, and
  Jesus crucified. Protection language in Scripture describes
  refuge and ultimate safety while acknowledging present harm. Trusting God is not
  a reason to ignore ordinary precaution; Proverbs praises the prudent person
  who sees danger and takes cover.

Scripture itself records a misuse of Psalm 91. Satan quotes the psalm as a
promise that Jesus can throw himself from the temple without injury. Jesus
refuses to put God to the test.

That exchange guides the rest of the topic. The images are places of refuge: a
tower to run toward, a shadow to rest under, and company through deep water.
They offer God's faithful presence, not a fence against every form of harm.
