shortAnswer: >-
  Wisdom in Scripture is practical skill for living well. Proverbs applies it
  to speech, money, work, friendship, and decisions. Its starting place is the
  fear of the Lord, a reverent understanding of who God is. James tells anyone
  who lacks wisdom to ask for it, while Proverbs also commends planning,
  careful judgment, and counsel from others.

misuse: >-
  Proverbs 3:5 is regularly used to discourage thinking, which cannot be right
  in a book that praises counsel, planning, evidence, and craftsmanship for
  thirty-one chapters. "Lean not on your own understanding" warns against
  treating personal judgment as final while still calling people to use it. Similarly,
  1 Corinthians 1:25 is quoted to shut down hard questions; Paul is contrasting
  the cross with the status-seeking wisdom of his culture. He isn't recommending
  ignorance. Another common error treats wisdom as a substitute for
  expertise. Scripture nowhere suggests that praying about a decision replaces
  seeking competent advice. Proverbs repeatedly locates
  safety in many advisers.
faqs:
  - q: What does "the fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom" mean?
    a: >-
      The phrase describes awe and reverence, taking God seriously enough for
      that reality to reorder other priorities.
      Proverbs 9:10 calls it the beginning, a starting point you keep returning
      to throughout a life of learning. The same books that commend this
      reverence also tell people not to live in terror.
  - q: How do I know God's will for a decision?
    a: >-
      James 1:5 gives the most direct answer: ask God for wisdom, which he gives
      generously without reproach. Proverbs 11:14 and 15:22 also locate safety
      in seeking many advisers, while James 3:17 tests advice by its character.
      Scripture places more emphasis on prayer, counsel, and wise conduct than
      on decoding signs.

Proverbs brings wisdom into ordinary decisions: how people speak, what they
sign, whose company they keep, and whether they finish their work. Wisdom is a
learnable skill for living.

That practical focus also clarifies Proverbs 3:5. The book's warning against
making personal understanding the final authority doesn't cancel thirty-one
chapters of careful judgment. It teaches humility within the work of thinking.
