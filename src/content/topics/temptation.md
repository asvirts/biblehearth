shortAnswer: >-
  Scripture clearly distinguishes temptation from sin: Hebrews says Jesus was
  tempted in every way and remained without sin. James also rules out God as
  the source of temptation. The Bible's practical counsel is simple and direct.
  Flee, stay alert, pray, and avoid the path that leads toward trouble. First
  Corinthians 10:13 promises an available way out, even when a person feels
  weak.

misuse: >-
  1 Corinthians 10:13 is the most misquoted verse on this page, usually as "God
  will never give you more than you can handle." It says nothing of the sort.
  The subject is temptation, and the promise is a way out. Paul himself wrote in
  2 Corinthians 1:8 that he was under pressure far beyond his ability to endure,
  which makes the popular reading impossible. Applied to grief, illness or
  abuse, this verse becomes cruel. The other error is treating the experience of
  temptation as guilt: Hebrews says Jesus was tempted in every way and did not
  sin, so the feeling itself can't be the offense.
faqs:
  - q: Does the Bible say God will not give you more than you can handle?
    a: >-
      No. That saying is a distortion of 1 Corinthians 10:13, which is about
      temptation and promises a way out of it. Paul
      wrote in 2 Corinthians 1:8 that he was under pressure far beyond his
      ability to endure, which rules out the popular reading. The verse is
      encouraging about temptation and doesn't set a limit on suffering.
  - q: Is being tempted a sin?
    a: >-
      No. Hebrews 4:15 says Jesus was tempted in every way we are yet was
      without sin, which requires temptation and sin to be different things.
      James 1:14-15 traces a sequence from desire through enticement to sin,
      with distinguishable steps. The experience of temptation isn't itself the
      offense.

The practical advice here is modest on purpose. Flee the situation. Avoid the
road that leads toward it. Pursue something better in the company of other
people. Scripture places wisdom and community ahead of heroic self-confidence.

The verse often used to make people feel weak is meant to encourage them.
Temptation is a common human experience, other people have faced it, and God
provides a door through which someone can leave.
