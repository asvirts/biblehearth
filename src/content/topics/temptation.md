shortAnswer: >-
  Scripture separates temptation from sin firmly enough that Hebrews can say
  Jesus was tempted in every way and remained without sin. James is equally
  firm that God is not the source of it. The practical advice is mostly
  unglamorous — flee, watch, pray, stay away from the setting — and 1
  Corinthians 10:13 promises a way out rather than a guarantee you will feel
  strong.

misuse: >-
  1 Corinthians 10:13 is the most misquoted verse on this page, usually as "God
  will never give you more than you can handle." It says nothing of the sort.
  The subject is temptation, and the promise is a way out — not that your
  circumstances will stay within your capacity. Paul himself wrote in
  2 Corinthians 1:8 that he was under pressure far beyond his ability to endure,
  which makes the popular reading impossible. Applied to grief, illness or
  abuse, this verse becomes cruel. The other error is treating the experience of
  temptation as guilt: Hebrews says Jesus was tempted in every way and did not
  sin, so the feeling itself cannot be the offence.
faqs:
  - q: Does the Bible say God will not give you more than you can handle?
    a: >-
      No. That saying is a distortion of 1 Corinthians 10:13, which is about
      temptation and promises a way out of it — not a limit on hardship. Paul
      wrote in 2 Corinthians 1:8 that he was under pressure far beyond his
      ability to endure, which rules out the popular reading. The verse is
      genuinely encouraging about temptation and says nothing about suffering.
  - q: Is being tempted a sin?
    a: >-
      No. Hebrews 4:15 says Jesus was tempted in every way we are yet was
      without sin, which requires temptation and sin to be different things.
      James 1:14-15 traces a sequence from desire through enticement to sin,
      with distinguishable steps — so the experience of temptation is not
      itself the offence.

The practical advice here is strikingly modest. Not resist heroically —
flee. Not stand your ground — avoid the road. Not manage it privately —
pursue something better in company with other people.

And the verse most often used to make people feel weak is doing the opposite
job. It says the pressure is ordinary, that others have faced it, and that
there is a door.
