title: Love

shortAnswer: >-
  The New Testament's central word for love, agape, describes commitment shown
  in action rather than affection felt. That is why it can be commanded, why
  1 Corinthians 13 defines it entirely with verbs, and why Jesus extends it to
  enemies. Scripture also consistently derives human love from a prior one —
  we love because he first loved us — rather than treating it as something
  summoned from scratch.

misuse: >-
  1 Corinthians 13 is read at weddings so often that its actual setting gets
  lost — Paul wrote it to a church quarrelling about whose spiritual gifts
  mattered most, as a rebuke. Read that way it is less romantic and more
  demanding. The larger misuse is treating love as identical to approval or to
  never confronting anything: Romans 12:9 attaches sincere love to hating what
  is evil, and Jesus was regularly blunt with people he loved. Love also is not
  a reason to stay in danger. "Love covers a multitude of sins" describes
  absorbing offence, not concealing abuse, and using it to silence someone is a
  straightforward inversion of the text.
faqs:
  - q: What is the greatest commandment in the Bible?
    a: >-
      In Matthew 22:37-39 Jesus names loving God with all your heart, soul and
      mind as the greatest, then immediately adds a second — loving your
      neighbour as yourself — and says all the Law and Prophets hang on the
      two. Notably he was asked for one and gave two, refusing to separate them.
  - q: What are the different Greek words for love in the Bible?
    a: >-
      The New Testament mainly uses agape, committed self-giving love, and
      philia, the love of friendship. Eros, romantic love, does not appear in
      the New Testament, and storge, family affection, appears only in compound
      forms. Agape is the word used in 1 Corinthians 13 and in the command to
      love enemies, which is why it can be commanded.
  - q: What does "God is love" actually mean?
    a: >-
      1 John 4:8 states it as a description of God's nature rather than one of
      his moods, which is why the same letter grounds our capacity to love in
      his having loved first. It does not mean love is God, or that whatever
      anyone calls love is divine — the same letter is sharply concerned with
      truth and with how love is demonstrated in action.

The word doing most of the work in these passages is agape, and it behaves
unlike the English word. It is commanded, extended to enemies, and defined by a
list of actions — none of which makes sense for an emotion.

That is what allows the New Testament to treat love as an obligation without
being absurd. You cannot be ordered to feel something. You can be ordered to be
patient, to stop keeping score, and to tell the truth.
