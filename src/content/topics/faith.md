shortAnswer: >-
  Hebrews describes faith as confidence in what is hoped for and assurance
  about what can't yet be seen. That definition leaves room for uncertainty.
  Ephesians calls faith a gift, and other passages show belief and doubt in the
  same person. Biblical faith also takes visible shape in the way a person
  lives and cares for others.

misuse: >-
  Matthew 17:20 is the most damaging misquotation here. The mustard seed is an
  image of how little faith is needed. It shouldn't be used to tell people that
  an unanswered prayer proves they didn't believe hard enough. That turns faith
  into currency and suffering into a verdict. Paul had remarkable faith and
  still wasn't healed after asking three times. Faith also allows room for
  thought and doubt. Hebrews 11 includes people who argued with God, and Mark
  9:24 records an answered prayer that openly admits unbelief. In Scripture,
  faith is trust in God, not confidence that one desired outcome will occur.
  That trust can endure even when events unfold differently.
faqs:
  - q: What is the biblical definition of faith?
    a: >-
      Hebrews 11:1 defines it as confidence in what is hoped for and assurance
      about what is not seen. The chapter leaves uncertainty within that
      definition: it later commends people who died without receiving what they
      had been promised.
  - q: Do faith and works contradict each other in the Bible?
    a: >-
      They address different questions. Paul argues in Ephesians 2:8-9 that
      salvation is not earned by works, answering how a person is put right
      with God. James argues in 2:17 that faith producing nothing is dead,
      answering how living faith becomes recognizable. Both reject a faith that
      never reaches beyond a claim.
  - q: Is it a sin to doubt?
    a: >-
      Scripture doesn't treat every doubt as sin. Mark 9:24 records a man confessing
      belief and unbelief in the same sentence and receiving what he asked for,
      Thomas is invited to inspect the evidence, and the Psalms are full of hard
      questions. Doubt brought honestly to God can be a form of engagement.

Hebrews 11 is often read as a hall of fame, but its ending complicates that
picture. The chapter says that the people it praises didn't receive everything
they were promised during their lifetimes.

The opening definition prepares readers for that ending. Faith here is trust
that continues without complete sight or immediate fulfillment. The chapter
commends people who lived by that trust while still waiting.
