shortAnswer: >-
  Scripture describes patience as something that grows. Galatians calls it
  fruit of the Spirit, and James connects it with the endurance formed through
  testing. Patience includes waiting through stubborn circumstances and bearing
  with difficult people. God's patience supplies both the example and the
  reason for practicing it.

misuse: >-
  Patience is one of the easiest virtues to weaponize. Telling someone to be
  patient with injustice misuses these texts. The same Scriptures
  contain prophets who denounce exploitation immediately and psalms that demand
  God act now. Patience in the New Testament is largely about bearing with
  people's ordinary failings and waiting for God; it doesn't require tolerating
  harm. "Wait on the Lord" also permits action. Isaiah 40 pairs
  waiting with renewed strength for continued activity, and Psalm 37 sets
  stillness against anxious fretting. Impatience isn't automatic proof of weak
  faith. The Psalms preserve many prayers that ask God, "How long?"
faqs:
  - q: Is patience a fruit of the Spirit?
    a: >-
      Yes. Galatians 5:22-23 lists patience among the fruit of the Spirit
      alongside love, joy, peace, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness
      and self-control. The agricultural image pictures fruit growing over time.
      Patience can be cultivated and isn't a fixed personality trait that a
      person either has or lacks.
  - q: What does the Bible say about waiting on God?
    a: >-
      Isaiah 40:31 promises renewed strength to those who wait, and Psalm 37:7
      pairs being still before God with not fretting over injustice. Both frame
      waiting as active and expectant. Romans 8:25 ties patience directly to
      hope for what isn't yet seen, giving the waiting a clear direction.

One word covers two different practices here. Patience waits for an answer, a
change, or a promise. It also bears with other people through the friction of
ordinary life, the form that appears most often in New Testament instructions.

Both forms can grow. Calling patience fruit means a person can cultivate it over
time. A hard day doesn't define someone's character or settle what growth is
possible.
