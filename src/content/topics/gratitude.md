  Scripture presents thanksgiving as a practice that can continue even when a
  grateful mood is absent. First Thessalonians says to give thanks *in* every
  circumstance; it doesn't ask people to call every circumstance good.
  Biblical gratitude often grows through memory, as people recall God's
  character and past actions.

misuse: >-
  The single most common error is reading 1 Thessalonians 5:18 as "give thanks
  for all circumstances." The verse says *in*. It allows gratitude to accompany
  suffering without requiring someone to be glad about the harm. Misusing the
  verse can pressure grieving or abused people to call their pain a gift, which
  Scripture never asks them to do. Gratitude also isn't a way to deny grief.
  The Bible commands thanksgiving while preserving Lamentations and psalms of
  complaint. Nor is gratitude a technique for getting more from God. Scripture
  grounds it in God's character and past care, not in a transaction.
faqs:
  - q: Does the Bible say to give thanks for everything?
    a: >-
      1 Thessalonians 5:18 says to give thanks *in* all circumstances, not
      *for* them. That wording asks for gratitude that persists through hardship
      without requiring anyone to call the hardship good. Ephesians 5:20 uses
      broader language, but the same Bible also contains Lamentations and
      psalms of unresolved complaint.
  - q: Why is gratitude linked to memory in the Bible?
    a: >-
      Psalm 103:2 has David telling his own soul not to forget God's benefits,
      and much of Israel's worship consists of reciting past events. The
      practice recognizes how easily people forget. Deliberate recollection can
      nourish gratitude before a thankful feeling arrives.

One small word protects the meaning of this teaching. Paul asks for thanks *in*
every circumstance. He leaves room to name grief, injustice, and pain without
calling them good.

Gratitude in Scripture grows through remembered details. People rehearse what
God has done, give thanks for specific gifts, and practice telling the truth
about goodness even on days when the feeling comes slowly.
