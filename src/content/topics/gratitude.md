shortAnswer: >-
  Scripture treats thanksgiving as a practice rather than a mood — something
  commanded, and therefore possible on days it is not felt. The instruction in
  1 Thessalonians is to give thanks *in* all circumstances, not *for* them, a
  distinction that matters enormously. Most biblical gratitude is anchored in
  memory: it works by recalling what happened, not by summoning a feeling.

misuse: >-
  The single most common error is reading 1 Thessalonians 5:18 as "give thanks
  for all circumstances." It says in — and the difference is the difference
  between company in suffering and being told to be glad about it. Used the
  wrong way, this verse becomes a way of telling grieving or abused people that
  their pain is a gift, which Scripture never does. Gratitude is also not a
  denial mechanism. The same collection that commands thanksgiving preserves
  Lamentations and the psalms of complaint, often in the same breath. And it is
  not a technique for getting more: Scripture grounds thanks in the giver's
  character, not in a transaction where gratitude unlocks further supply.
faqs:
  - q: Does the Bible say to give thanks for everything?
    a: >-
      1 Thessalonians 5:18 says to give thanks *in* all circumstances, not
      *for* them. That preposition is doing a great deal of work: it asks for
      gratitude that persists through hardship without requiring anyone to call
      the hardship good. Ephesians 5:20 is broader, but the same collection
      contains Lamentations and psalms of unresolved complaint.
  - q: Why is gratitude linked to memory in the Bible?
    a: >-
      Psalm 103:2 has David telling his own soul not to forget God's benefits,
      and much of Israel's worship consists of reciting past events. The
      assumption is that forgetting is the default state and that gratitude is
      produced by deliberate recollection rather than by waiting to feel
      thankful.

One preposition carries most of this topic. Paul asks for thanks *in* all
circumstances, and the version people quote — thanks *for* all circumstances —
is a different and much crueller instruction that he never gives.

Held correctly, gratitude in Scripture is closer to remembering than to
feeling. It is the discipline of saying out loud what has already happened.
