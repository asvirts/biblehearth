shortAnswer: >-
  Work in Scripture predates the fall — Genesis puts the first human in a garden
  to tend it before anything goes wrong — so it is not framed as a punishment.
  What changes is difficulty. Alongside the commands to work diligently sits an
  equally firm command to stop, and the sabbath is the part most likely to be
  skipped by people quoting the rest.

misuse: >-
  Colossians 3:23 — work at it with all your heart, as working for the Lord —
  is regularly used by employers to extract more from people, and by individuals
  to justify overwork. It was written to enslaved people in a Roman household as
  a way of preserving dignity in labour they did not choose, not as a
  productivity charge. Read alongside the sabbath command it cannot mean
  unlimited effort. 2 Thessalonians 3:10 is the other common weapon: Paul writes
  about those *unwilling* to work in a specific community that had downed tools
  awaiting the second coming, and applying it to people who cannot work, or
  cannot find work, inverts a body of Scripture that is fiercely protective of
  the poor.
faqs:
  - q: Is work a punishment for sin in the Bible?
    a: >-
      No. Genesis 2:15 places the first human in the garden to work and keep it
      before the fall, so labour is part of the original arrangement. What
      changes in Genesis 3 is difficulty — the ground resists, and work becomes
      toilsome. The activity is not the curse; the frustration attached to it is.
  - q: What does the Bible say about rest and burnout?
    a: >-
      The fourth commandment devotes more words to stopping than to working and
      extends rest to employees, foreigners and animals, which makes it a matter
      of justice rather than preference. Psalm 127:2 directly challenges anxious
      overwork, and Jesus says in Mark 2:27 that the sabbath was made for people
      rather than people for the sabbath.

The sequence in Genesis is easy to miss and changes the whole topic. Work is
assigned in chapter two, in the garden, before anything has gone wrong. The
curse in chapter three does not create work; it makes the ground fight back.

The other half is the one usually left out. The command to labour six days is
also a command to stop on the seventh, and it applies to everyone in the
household — including the people who have no say in the matter.
