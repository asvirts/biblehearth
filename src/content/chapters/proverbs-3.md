  Proverbs 3 teaches trust in the Lord through remembered instruction, humble
  dependence, generosity, and practical care for neighbors. Its familiar promise
  about straight paths appears between two warnings against self-reliance. The
  chapter describes the usual blessings of wisdom while also preparing readers for
  loving discipline. Wisdom shaped creation itself and becomes visible in ordinary

faqs:
  - q: What does "trust in the LORD with all your heart" mean?
    a: >-
      Trust means placing the weight of one's decisions and direction on God with the
      expectation that he is reliable. "With all your heart" calls for an undivided
      commitment. The next line contrasts this dependence with making personal
      understanding the final support.
  - q: What does "lean not on your own understanding" mean?
    a: >-
      "Lean not on your own understanding" does not reject thought; Proverbs
      repeatedly encourages people to gain understanding. It rejects treating
      personal judgment as the final authority.
      Verse 7 restates the concern by warning against being wise in one's own eyes.
      Mature wisdom combines careful thought with humility before God.
  - q: What does "he will make your paths straight" mean?
    a: >-
      The Hebrew is closer to "level" or "make passable" than to "reveal". Some
      translations say "direct your paths", which can sound like a promise of specific decision guidance.
      The roadbuilding image more directly promises God's care in making faithful
      progress possible.
  - q: Does Proverbs 3:9-10 promise financial blessing for giving?
    a: >-
      Proverbs describes general patterns of wise living instead of guarantees for
      every person. Barns and vats picture an abundant harvest for an agricultural
      audience. The next two verses prepare readers for loving discipline, showing
      that generosity can coexist with a difficult season. The passage should not be
      used as a formula for financial gain.
  - q: What does "honor the LORD with your firstfruits" mean?
    a: >-
      Firstfruits were the earliest and best produce, offered before the rest of the
      harvest was consumed or sold. Giving first acknowledged God as the source and
      required trust before the season's final outcome was known. The principle
      continues to shape thoughtful generosity beyond farming.
  - q: What does "do not despise the LORD's discipline" mean?
    a: >-
      Discipline means correction intended to train. The passage warns against
      dismissing it or becoming embittered by it, then compares God's action with a
      loving father who delights in his child. Hardship does not automatically signal
      the withdrawal of God's favor.
  - q: What does "do not withhold good from your neighbor" mean?
    a: >-
      The command includes unnecessary delay. When good is owed and the means are
      available, a person should not say "come back tomorrow". Wages,
      repayment, or promised help all fit the principle. Wisdom acts promptly when
      another person's well-being is at stake.

## Reading Proverbs 3 as a whole

Verses 5 through 7 belong together. Trust in the Lord and the promise of straight
paths sit between the warnings "Lean not on your own understanding" and "be not wise
in your own eyes". The passage calls capable people toward humble dependence as much
as it comforts people who feel uncertain.

Verses 11 and 12 also shape the earlier promises. Long life, health, favor, and full
barns describe wisdom's normal fruit, while loving correction remains part of the
relationship. A difficult season does not prove that a faithful person has been
abandoned.

The chapter reaches its widest view in verses 19 and 20, where God creates by wisdom.
It then returns to familiar responsibilities between neighbors. The same wisdom that
orders the world guides timely payment, peaceful relationships, and a refusal to envy
violence.
