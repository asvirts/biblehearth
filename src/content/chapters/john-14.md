shortAnswer: >-
  John 14 records Jesus comforting his disciples on the night before his arrest. He
  promises to prepare a place for them and says, "I am the way and the truth and the
  life." He identifies himself as the way to the Father,
  and says that seeing him reveals the Father. He also promises the Holy Spirit, who
  will teach and remain with them. The chapter begins and ends with a call to troubled
  hearts, grounded in Jesus' continuing presence and peace.

themes:
  - name: Jesus welcomes honest questions
    explanation: >-
      Thomas, Philip, and Judas each interrupt Jesus with a question. Their confusion
      leads to some of the chapter's clearest teaching about Jesus, the Father, and
      God's presence with believers. The setting matters: Jesus gives these answers
      to frightened friends a few hours before his arrest.
  - name: Jesus prepares a home and makes a home
    explanation: >-
      The same Greek word for a dwelling appears in verses 2 and 23, its only two New
      Testament uses. Jesus prepares a place for the disciples in his Father's house,
      and the Father and Son make their home with the person who loves Jesus. Future
      belonging and present fellowship are linked by the shared word.
  - name: Troubled hearts receive reasons for trust
    explanation: >-
      Jesus repeats "Do not let your hearts be troubled" in verses 1 and 27, adding
      "do not be afraid" the second time. Between those commands he gives reasons for
      trust: a prepared place, his return, the Spirit's presence, fellowship with the
      Father and Son, and his peace.
  - name: His promises center on presence
    explanation: >-
      The disciples want to know where Jesus is going and why they cannot follow yet.
      Jesus responds with promises that they will not be abandoned. He will return,
      the Spirit will remain with them, and the Father and Son will make their home
      with them. This emphasis explains the chapter's lasting place in times of grief.
  - name: It ends in motion
    explanation: >-
      Jesus closes with "Get up! Let us go on from here." The final words, "let us go
      on from here", move toward the garden where
      he will be arrested. The peace he offers in verse 27 comes from someone fully
      aware of the approaching danger and committed to obeying the Father through it.
glossary:
  - term: Advocate
    plain: >-
      The Greek word *parakletos* describes someone called alongside to help or speak
      on another's behalf. Translations use Advocate, Helper, Comforter, or Counselor.
      By promising "another" Advocate, Jesus connects the Spirit's ministry with his
      own care for the disciples.
  - term: many rooms
    plain: >-
      The King James Version uses "mansions," which in 1611 meant dwelling places.
      The Greek refers to places to stay within the Father's household, with room for
      everyone Jesus brings there.
  - term: in my name
    plain: >-
      To ask as Jesus' representative and in keeping with his purposes, as an envoy
      acts with a ruler's authority. Jesus connects such prayer with the Father's
      glory.
  - term: the Spirit of truth
    plain: >-
      Jesus' title for the Holy Spirit, who will teach the disciples and remind them
      of Jesus' words. The title describes the Spirit's character and teaching work.
  - term: the prince of this world
    plain: >-
      John's phrase for the devil as a ruler exercising influence in the present
      world. Jesus says this ruler is coming but has no rightful claim over him.
  - term: orphans
    plain: >-
      Children without parents, protection, or reliable provision. Jesus uses this
      family word to name the abandonment his adult disciples fear when he leaves.

caution: >-
  Verses 13 and 14 do not function as a blank check for any request followed by the
  phrase "in Jesus' name." Such prayer represents Jesus' purposes and aims at the
      Father's glory, "so that the Father may be glorified in the Son". The "greater
      works" in verse 12 are tied to Jesus' return to the
  Father and the Spirit-empowered spread of the gospel, so they should not become a
  promise of miracles on demand. Verse 6 makes an exclusive claim that access to the
  Father comes through Jesus. It first answers a frightened disciple who admits he is
  lost, and should be explained with the same patient care.
faqs:
  - q: What does "I am the way, the truth, and the life" mean?
    a: >-
      Thomas asks how the disciples can know the route when they do not know Jesus'
      destination. Jesus identifies himself as the route. He provides access to the
      Father, reveals the truth about God, and gives divine life. The second half of
      the verse states the conclusion: no one comes to the Father except through him.
  - q: Does John 14:6 mean everyone who is not a Christian is lost?
    a: >-
      John 14:6 says access to the Father comes through Jesus. Christians broadly
      agree that salvation is through Christ alone, then differ on how that applies to
      people who never hear his name, infants, and people under the old covenant. The
      verse rules out treating Jesus as one independent route among many, while leaving
      the judgment of particular people to God.
  - q: What are the many rooms or mansions in my Father's house?
    a: >-
      Jesus says, "In my Father's house are many rooms". They are dwelling places
      within God's household. In 1611, the King James word
      "mansions" meant places to stay, and the Greek carries that sense. Verse 23 uses
      the same Greek word when Jesus says they will "make our home with him", linking
      the future home with God's present presence.
  - q: Does "whatever you ask in my name" mean God grants every prayer?
    a: >-
      Asking in Jesus' name means praying as his representative, in line with his
      character and purposes. Verse 13 gives the aim: the Father will be glorified in
      the Son. Adding a phrase to a request does not guarantee a yes. Elsewhere, Paul
      describes asking three times for something and receiving a different answer.
  - q: Who is the Helper or Paraclete Jesus promised?
    a: >-
      Jesus identifies the Helper as the Holy Spirit in verse 26. *Parakletos* means
      someone called alongside to help or advocate, which explains the varied English
      translations. The Spirit will continue Jesus' care, stay with the disciples
      forever, teach them, and live in them.
  - q: Why does Jesus say "the Father is greater than I"?
    a: >-
      This sentence stood at the center of early Christian debates. Arius used it to
      argue that the Son was a lesser being, while the councils behind the Nicene
      Creed taught that Father and Son share one divine nature within an ordered
      relationship. The mainstream reading applies the statement to Jesus' humble,
      obedient mission. The same chapter says that seeing Jesus reveals the Father and
      that each dwells in the other.
  - q: What does "whoever has seen me has seen the Father" mean?
    a: >-
      Jesus tells Philip that the Father is revealed through the Son. Verse 10
      explains their relationship: Jesus is in the Father, the Father is in him, and
      the Father works through his words and deeds. Philip has asked for a separate
      vision of God after years of seeing that revelation in Jesus.
  - q: What does "whoever believes will do greater works than these" mean?
    a: >-
      Jesus ties the greater works to his return to the Father and, in John's wider
      account, the Spirit's arrival. The common reading sees the gospel spreading after
      Pentecost across a wider area and among more people than Jesus' earthly ministry
      reached. Some readers also include the miracles recorded in Acts. The verse does
      not guarantee a particular miracle to each believer.

## Reading John 14 in its setting

Jesus speaks these words on the night of his arrest. He has eaten with the disciples,
washed their feet, and announced a betrayal. The chapter ends as he gets up to move
toward the danger he has already described.

Three questions shape the conversation. Thomas admits that he does not know the way,
and Jesus answers by identifying himself as the way, truth, and life. Philip asks to
see the Father, and Jesus explains that the Father has been revealed in him. Judas,
the disciple other than Iscariot, asks about Jesus' self-revelation, leading to the
promise that the Father and Son will make their home with believers.

The chapter begins and ends with the words "do not let your hearts be troubled".
Jesus fills the space
between with promises of continued presence: he will return, the disciples will not
be orphans, the Spirit will remain, and the Father and Son will dwell with them. His
peace meets them before they fully understand what is about to happen.
