title: "John 15: The Vine, the Branches, and a Life of Love"
shortAnswer: >-
  In John 15, Jesus compares himself to a vine and his disciples to branches that
  bear fruit by remaining in him. He calls them to remain in his love and love one
  another, then prepares them for opposition. The chapter ends with the Spirit of
  truth and the disciples bearing witness to him.

    explanation: >-

  - name: Love becomes visible among friends
    explanation: >-
      Jesus calls the disciples friends and tells them to love one another as he

  - name: Fruit, pruning, and a warning
    explanation: >-
      The gardener prunes a fruitful branch for greater fruitfulness, while the

      Jesus seriously and gives the Father's care a central place.
  - name: Witness in the face of opposition
    explanation: >-
      Jesus moves from love within the group to hatred they may meet outside it.
      He does not leave the disciples to speak alone: the Spirit of truth will
      testify, and those who have been with Jesus will also bear witness.

caution: >-
  Jesus uses severe language about a branch that does not remain in him. Christians
  differ on whether it warns that a believer can fall away or exposes a connection
  that was outward rather than enduring. The warning should not be dismissed, and
  one difficult season cannot tell an observer the final state of another person.
  The chapter directs disciples toward remaining in Jesus and loving one another;
  it does not authorize using suffering as proof that God is punishing someone.
faqs:
  - q: What does it mean to abide in Jesus in John 15?
    a: >-
      To abide means to remain or continue. In John 15 Jesus connects remaining
      in him with his words remaining in the disciples, keeping his commands, and
      remaining in his love. Prayer, listening to his teaching, and loving others
      are ways Christians seek to live in that continuing relationship.
  - q: What does Jesus mean by the true vine?
    a: >-
      Jesus calls himself the true vine, his Father the gardener, and his
      disciples the branches. A branch receives life from the vine on which it
      grows. The image shows the disciples' dependence on Jesus for the fruit he
      calls them to bear. Vine imagery also appears for God's people in Israel's
      Scriptures, including Isaiah 5.
  - q: What is the difference between pruning and cutting off?
    a: >-
      In John 15:2, the gardener removes a branch that bears no fruit and prunes a
      fruitful one so it can bear more. Pruning is care directed toward growth;
      removal is a warning within the image. The passage does not identify every
      painful event in a person's life as a particular act of pruning.
  - q: Do the branches burned in John 15 mean salvation can be lost?
    a: >-
      Christians disagree. Some see a real warning that a believer can fall away,
      because the branch is described as connected to the vine and then removed.
      Others understand the warning as exposing a merely outward connection,
      drawing on passages that emphasize God's keeping power. John 15 urges readers
      to remain in Jesus without spelling out every question in the later debate.
  - q: What does apart from me you can do nothing mean?
    a: >-
      Jesus is speaking about bearing fruit as his disciples, using a branch that
      cannot produce grapes when separated from the vine. He is not denying that
      people can accomplish ordinary tasks without professing faith. The image
      emphasizes dependence on him for the life and fruit he describes.
  - q: Does John 15:7 promise that God will grant any request?
    a: >-
      Jesus links the promise to his disciples remaining in him and his words
      remaining in them. The surrounding passage speaks of fruitfulness and the
      Father's glory. Christians understand the invitation to pray within that
      relationship, not as a way to command any outcome they desire.
  - q: What does greater love has no one than this mean?
    a: >-
      In John 15:13 Jesus describes laying down one's life for friends as the
      greatest expression of love. He speaks shortly before his own death and
      commands the disciples to love one another as he has loved them. His example
      gives their care for one another a self-giving shape.
  - q: Why does Jesus say the world will hate his followers?
    a: >-
      Jesus warns that those who oppose him may also oppose his disciples because
      they belong to him and carry his message. He tells them this after calling
      them to love one another. The warning prepares them for rejection; it does
      not make every disagreement or criticism proof of persecution.

## If this is your first time reading John 15

Notice how the picture of a branch opens into a conversation about love. Jesus
first says that a branch bears fruit by remaining in the vine. Then he says to
remain in his love and gives the disciples a concrete command: love one another.

In verse 10, Jesus connects remaining in his love with keeping his commands.
The command he names in verse 12 is to love one another as he has loved them.
In verse 14, he calls those who do what he commands his friends. He explains
that friendship in verse 15 by saying he has shared what he heard from the Father.

Jesus also reminds these disciples that he chose and appointed them to bear
lasting fruit. In this setting, his words speak directly to the people he sends;
Christians differ over how the choosing relates to broader questions about salvation.

The chapter does not end at the vineyard. Jesus expects opposition, and he
promises the Spirit of truth will testify about him. The disciples, who have
been with him, will also tell what they have seen and heard.
