shortAnswer: >-
  Psalm 121 is a pilgrim song about God's constant care. A traveler looks toward the
  hills and asks where help will come from, then answers: from the Lord, maker of
  heaven and earth. A second voice responds with six uses of the Hebrew word for
  keeping or guarding. God never sleeps, remains close through day and night, and
  watches over the whole journey. The psalm promises an attentive keeper in a world
  where travel still involves real danger.

      travelers confidence in God's uninterrupted attention without denying the real

    a: >-

      believers will never stumble in any sense.

    a: >-

      also suggests an ally standing close enough to support and defend. Together,
      the images describe practical relief and personal nearness.
  - q: What is a Song of Ascents?
    a: >-
      Psalms 120 through 134 share this heading. The common explanation is that
      pilgrims sang them while going up to Jerusalem for festivals. Other proposals
      connect the title with temple steps or the poems' structure. Psalm 121's feet,
      sun, shade, and coming and going fit the travel setting well.

## Reading Psalm 121 as a pilgrim song

The heading places this short poem among the Songs of Ascents, commonly connected
with journeys to Jerusalem. Verse 1 looks toward the hills and asks where help will
come from. The landscape can carry several associations, including rough travel,
hilltop shrines, and the road ahead. Verse 2 gives the answer in every case: help
comes from the Lord who made heaven and earth.

The pronouns change after verse 2. One voice has spoken of "my help", and another now
speaks about "your foot", "your keeper", and "your coming and going". The response
is three times as long as the question and sounds well suited to travelers encouraging
one another.

The Hebrew word for keeping appears six times, though English versions translate it
as "your Protector", "your keeper", "will guard you", "will preserve", and "will
watch over". This repetition concentrates the psalm on God's attentive care. The
keeper of Israel does not sleep, remains near through day and night, and watches over
the whole life of the traveler.
